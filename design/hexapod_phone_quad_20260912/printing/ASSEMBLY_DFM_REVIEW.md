# R4 assembly and print review

Use the R4 model and the selected stacks in [R4_FASTENER_STACKS.md](R4_FASTENER_STACKS.md). The R2 and R3 reviews and hardware estimates are preserved under `../manufacturing/baseline_r2/printing/` and `../manufacturing/baseline_r3/printing/`. Their auxiliary seating dimensions and several screw lengths no longer apply.

The corrected C018 drawing datum places the output axis 12.5 mm from the case center. The 45.23 mm case therefore extends from X-35.115 to X10.115 in the servo frame. R4 derives the clamps from the shared `cad/servo_contract.py`, reverses the knee case toward the hip, and moves the femur closure to X65, Z20.5. The hip retains its outward orientation. These changes alter clamp screw access and moving mass positions even though the joint centers remain unchanged.

The load path still runs from the curved toe through the tibia bridge and both cheeks, through the supplied metal discs and knee case, then through the femur, hip, supported yaw arm and deck. Keep material around bridge junctions, clamp lands and nut supports before hollowing broad faces. R3's larger bridges, full-thickness external-nut faces, edge ribs, thicker lower yaw arm and staggered coxa nut windows remain part of the R4 structure. The battery tray's sloping tab ribs and the phone saddle's retained nut roofs also remain.

The corrected auxiliary disc face is servo Z-32.4. Four integral 1.1 mm lands connect each pitch auxiliary cheek to that face; each yaw spacer is 11.6 mm long. Pitch auxiliary screws change to M3x8 with two thin washers. Yaw auxiliary screws remain M3x20 with two thin washers and a deeper 3.5 mm counterbore. Both arrangements leave only 0.2 mm nominal margin behind the 2.1 mm flange. Those small margins require checking the actual discs, washers, printed seating and screw tips.

An added OD7 washer under each supplied PA3.0x5 auxiliary-center retainer spans the disc's 6.05 mm bore and bears against the fixed stub. The washer has 0.475 mm radial overlap and leaves 0.7 mm nominal axial gap to the disc face. Check the printed central passage, actual washer diameter and free disc rotation. Nominal 4.5 mm screw entry beyond the washer does not establish the real self-tapping pilot depth or engagement.

Build one leg first. Insert each captured nut through its intended opening, fit the servo and its supplied discs, and attach the cheeks independently. Install the added center-retention washers before a cheek prevents access. Load the coxa's staggered nuts before closing the lower arm. The knee clamp screws now enter from below, opposite to the hip. Confirm each driver can reach its screw with the adjacent links installed. Check exposed bridge nuts, heads and tips throughout the permitted motion with torque disabled. A hole being open in CAD is not proof that a head, nut, tool or loaded washer can be installed and retained.

For printing, broad-cheek orientations preserve the principal bending paths within layers, while integrated offsets still need local support. A 45 degree orientation must earn its place through sliced layer and adhesion checks. It cannot be selected from face angles alone, which can miss floating islands. Remove support from contact faces and keep the same material, orientation and wall settings between critical coupons and their corresponding parts.

The R4 distributed hardware model counts 225 screws, including 25 supplied retainers, 100 nuts and 256 washers. Its estimated range is 273.946 to 390.991 g, with 334.673 g nominal. The conservative scenario replaces the old 50 g fastener allowance and allocates mass to the actual owning links. Weigh the assembled hardware and confirm whether the servo specification includes the supplied center screws before loaded qualification. The model supplies mass and inertia estimates; physical fit, printed strength, free rotation and walking still require their separate checks.
