# R4 print and assembly guide

Start with PRINT_FIRST.txt and FIRST_FIT_CHECKS.txt. R4 is cleared for controlled first-fit printing after the digital geometry, phone-access and staged clamp-nut checks. Full production remains gated on physical fixtures and one-leg tests. The printed structure has not been qualified. The Mi9SE remains landscape, 147.5 mm across and 70.5 mm high, screen upright. USB is accessible beside either short edge while pan is parked.

The output axis is 12.5 mm from the CASE MIDPOINT, not from an end. In the servo-local frame the corrected case spans X=-35.115 to 10.115 mm. The knee case now points inward and its clamp screws enter from below. Cables leave the auxiliary face; the supplier shell is a compatible reference, not proof of exact delivered-unit identity. Confirm the case, horn stack and plugged cable in the combined gauge before a full leg.

## Print the essential set first

The plate manifest labels each plate first_print or optional. The first-print set has 16 pieces: 13 fit pieces and three section specimens required before loading. Fit pieces comprise the nominal case saddle and shared cap, the new case-to-shaft registration plate, two horn-fixture cheeks and 38 mm bridge, the nominal 11.6 mm yaw spacer and hole gauge, plus the nominal phone tray, two clips, USB spine and hub. The three structural specimens are the corrected top-bridge main and auxiliary sections and staggered post. Their source mandatory flags remain visible in coupon_bom.csv; they are included here because the later load-test gate requires them.

The twelve optional pieces cover 0.2 and 0.4 mm case end clearances, their duplicate caps, 11.3 and 11.9 mm yaw spacers, and 0.3 and 0.7 mm phone fits with two clips each. One cap fits all case end-clearance trials, so its two duplicates need not be printed. All alternatives remain available individually through coupon_bom.csv. Optional plates include the duplicates for an explicit print-everything route. A passing hub coupon can replace the identical production hub. Cropped sections and shortened trays cannot replace complete production parts.

These are geometry-only 3MF files in millimetres, with named objects and matching combined STL files. Preserve the supplied orientations and positions. They contain no machine profile or executable G-code. The packer uses at most 215 × 215 mm of geometry on the 225 mm bed and leaves at least 8 mm between object bounding boxes. Confirm support, brim and skirt also fit. Print the deck and cover individually; their approximate 206 and 208 mm spans become 214 and 216 mm with a 4 mm brim on each side.

## Slicer setup and evidence

Use the actual Neptune 4 Pro configuration. R4 production analysis used a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, 0.42 mm line width, five walls, six top and bottom layers, 35% gyroid, 210/60 °C PLA and an 8 mm³/s flow ceiling. Starting speeds remain 20 mm/s first layer, 35 outer walls, 60 inner walls and 80 infill, subject to the flow ceiling. Use full cooling after layer three. Adjust temperature to the filament maker's range.

The recorded support is normal zigzag at 15%, with a 60% roof, 0.6 mm roof thickness, 0.2 mm Z separation and 0.5 mm XY clearance. These are an analysis profile and starting settings, not measured printer calibration.

[R4 production slicing](../manufacturing/slicing_r4/output/PRODUCTION_SLICING.md) covers all 34 SKUs and 76 production pieces. Estimated model PLA is 1.047638 kg, removable support 50.96 g and adhesion 1.16 g. The sum of individually sliced production jobs is 104.1 predicted hours; it is not the time for the first-print plate. Thirty-five percent infill does not mean 35% of solid mass. Coupon plates need their own machine-specific slice, and physical material usage and time remain measurements.

Preserve the R4 print STLs. The coxa now uses +90° about X, superseding the old opposite orientation. Leg cheeks keep their broad outer faces on the bed and ribs above. Inspect support beneath offset bridges, clamp features and the curved foot. The cover is inverted, with structural ribs and local nut features; inspect the current support toolpaths rather than carrying forward the R3 support estimate. The battery tray has sloping ribs beneath its raised tabs; preserve the exported orientation and inspect tab starts and the floor in the layer preview. Do not delete ribs to reduce support.

The phone frame prints on its column backs at X=-8.5, with removable build-plate support beneath the central rear plate at X=-4.5. Its top cap prints inverted. Phone-fit coupons use the same reinforced-back orientation. Bridge the pan saddle's short roofed nut pockets without trapping internal support. Inspect continuous toolpaths around holes, roof corners and rib starts. Keep removal scars off horn seats and washer lands. Material or orientation changes require new coupons; PLA settings and strength assumptions do not transfer unchanged to PETG.

## Fasteners

The [R4 fastener stacks](R4_FASTENER_STACKS.md) and fastener_mass.csv give attachment-level quantities. The BOM requires 200 separately purchased screws: 52 M3×6, 32 M3×8, 20 M3×10, 16 M3×12, eight M3×16, 24 M3×20, 36 M3×35 and twelve M3×50. Add thirteen supplied main M3×6 and twelve supplied auxiliary PA3.0×5 retainers. It also requires 100 plain M3 nuts and 256 washers, comprising 148 OD6 and 108 OD7, all ID3.2 and 0.5 mm thick. Plain nuts are nominally 5.5 mm across flats and 2.4 mm thick. Counts exclude spares and extra fixtures. Inventory does not prove every size is present.

Use matching metric drivers, a slim socket or small spanner, caliper and deburring tools. Check nuts and washers before loading pockets. Do not force taller nylocs into plain-nut pockets.

## Assembly and fixture loop

Preload all three clamp nuts into each bare carrier before fitting its servo, cap or neighboring links. The two front hip-clamp nuts need a staged side-entry route while the integrated post stays present and the lower arm and femur remain off. In raw servo coordinates, hold each at X=13.915 and Y=-20 to -17.6. Feed the lower nut along Z=-40 to -23 and the upper nut along Z=+5 to -7, then shift either nut +3.9 mm in Y into its seat. The other seven clamp nuts per leg enter axially from outside their bare carriers. Nominal flat clearance in the checked route is only 0.1 mm, so test the actual print and nuts without forcing them. Seven interference pairs remain for nut service in the fully assembled model; remove the surrounding femur or lower arm access obstructions before replacement. See the staged assembly check in the CAD output for the verified paths.

1. Print the first-print set and record material, layers, dimensions and verdict in coupon_feedback.csv. Case labels vary end clearance only. Preload the nominal saddle's three nuts, insert the real servo and fit the cap loosely with M3×35 screws. Fit the actual main horn, then attach the registration plate to the saddle's X9, Y±20 feet with two M3×12 bolts, two nuts and four OD6 washers. Seat the four horn screws by hand. Case, frame and horn must meet without pulling a misaligned part into position. Only then tighten the cap evenly until it locates the case, with closure travel remaining. There is no established tightening torque. Reject lift, distortion or cap bottoming. If the horn face misses the plate axially, measure seating height; do not change the longitudinal shaft datum to compensate. Support the gauge's two offset feet in its inverted print orientation.

2. Fit the real metal discs to the cheek coupons and 38 mm bridge. The main spline boss faces the servo. Verify centre-tool access and free auxiliary rotation with retention installed. Use the nominal 11.6 mm yaw spacer first. Compare optional 11.3 and 11.9 mm spacers only if measurements require adjustment. Fit the auxiliary cheek's integral 1.1 mm lands and M3×8 screws with both thin washers; verify free movement before bridge closure.

3. Assemble the R4 top-bridge sections with an M3×50, external nut and two OD7 washers. Check full cheek thickness beneath the washers, indentation, cracking and separation during gentle seating. Load the post's two nuts through opposite staggered windows. The cropped post ends at Z=-28 and tests nut-window assembly only; it excludes the critical clamp-access cut near Z=-25. Print the complete production coxa and lower yaw arm for the structural fixture, retaining the actual upper yaw plate, lower arm, discs and screws. Passing the cropped specimen cannot qualify the complete post.

4. Build one complete leg with torque disabled. Preload the yaw and hip clamp nuts using the bare-carrier routes above, then fit the yaw clamp, upper coxa, hip servo and cap. Install the yaw auxiliary disc, OD7 centre washer and supplied PA retainer before fitting the removable lower arm; its 7 mm central opening is for driver access. Load both coxa post nuts, then install measured spacers, four M3×20 auxiliary screws with paired OD6 washers, and both M3×20 post screws. Pitch auxiliary cheeks have 7.8 mm central openings, leaving 0.4 mm nominal radial clearance around their OD7 centre washers. Check free rotation after every small tightening increment.

5. Preload the knee-clamp nuts in the bare femur carrier, then fit the femur main cheek and inward-pointing knee servo. Its cap screws enter from below, opposite to the hip. Complete the clamp screws before surrounding links obstruct service access, then fit the separate auxiliary cheek. Close its bridge using the external nut stack. Fit the curved tibia and its auxiliary cheek, then both transverse bolts. Inspect screw heads, tips and wires throughout the intended restricted sweep. Do not spring a closed yoke over a servo or hammer a fit.

6. Put the leg in a captured fixture. Establish a low-force baseline, then apply incremental vertical and lateral forces, recording lever arm, tip displacement, twist and residual set after unloading. Test both lateral directions because reversing loads can open a bridge and change load sharing. Use the historical R3 limit of 43 Nmm, approximately 0.43 N at 100 mm, only as an initial conservative fixture-planning ceiling pending R4 section results; begin below it and retain the actual sagittal-load context. This is a fixture-planning bound, not a walking rating. Stop at cracking, whitening, slip, progressive deflection or permanent set. Do not begin with a 5 N side push plus maximum vertical load.

7. Increase vertical loading toward the measured standing reaction only after repeatable low-load behavior. For the coxa, apply vertical load at the actual hip saddle, X=50, while retaining the post near X=32 and both yaw support paths. Test independently reversible hip reaction torque about Y. A fully isolated post torque test is a conservative bound; the complete frame test determines load sharing. The ordinary three-foot vertical-only screen has margin above two, but assigning the full 0.588 N·m hip torque to the access-cut post, or combining 2g and a 5 N side force, remains an unresolved weakness. Do not infer a torque limit from the vertical result. The historical 12 N and 24 N screening targets belong on sacrificial, captured specimens with incremental loading. Review each result before increasing force and repeat the coupon and one-leg loop after any geometry, material or process change.

8. Fit the deck, ribbed battery tray and measured electronics, routing straps and wiring before closing the cover. Follow WIRING.md. Mount the pan saddle with its four cover spacers and tighten the four M3×16 body screws. Slide the cap nuts inward through outward-facing slots at Z80.9 to 83.6, beneath 2.9 mm roofs. Insert all four before fitting the servo cap. Tighten its four screws evenly until the lips contact the case with the intended 0.3 mm ear travel remaining. Observe every roof for lift, cracks and nut rotation. Complete both the body and servo-cap screw stages before installing the horn or hub; later parts obstruct those driver paths.

9. Confirm the pan body and servo-cap screw stages are complete, then fit the horn, centre retainer, hub and feet. Load frame post nuts through the 7.4 mm rear access wells at the unchanged M3×16 stack plane. Load feet nuts from the phone side before insertion. Fit both short lower clips and a dimensionally representative dummy phone, then the top cap. Check the USB-spine coupon, parked plug, camera openings and button relief.

10. With the dummy secured in a captured fixture, apply small measured pushes and record column deflection and residual set. The 5 N phone push is an analytical case, not permission to apply it immediately. Never use the frame as a handle. Insert the real Mi9SE only after these checks, without pressure on glass or buttons. Use Wi-Fi and its battery during pan; park and disable pan before side USB charging.

11. Record clamp movement and deflection after an hour and a day. Repeat relevant checks at the measured servo-adjacent operating temperature before prolonged standing. Only after reviewing these results should a supported, low-speed, level-floor full-robot test begin, with immediate servo-power removal available.

## Limits of the evidence

The corrected R4 interface is a digital design result. Physical case-to-shaft fit, screw engagement, free auxiliary rotation, plug clearance, printed strength and loaded motion remain separate prerequisites. The R3 baseline is preserved in manufacturing/baseline_r3; its mass, motion and section results do not qualify the corrected R4 geometry.

The R4 fastener model estimates 0.274 to 0.391 kg, nominally 0.335 kg. The source-matched [mass comparison](assembled_mass_comparison.json) gives 2.748544 kg for the deposited-material assembly and 2.911449 kg for the full-solid comparison. Weigh actual hardware and printed parts. The post's ordinary three-foot vertical-only screen has margin above two, but its clamp-access cut has not been qualified for the full hip torque or combined disturbance load. Keep the complete frame's load-sharing test as a release gate. Deposited mass and infill percentage do not prove strength, and there is no measured warm, fatigue or creep rating.
