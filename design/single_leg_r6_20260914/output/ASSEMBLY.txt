# Assemble one complete leg

This set makes one three-servo leg and its bench mounting plate. The servo pockets retain the fitted dimensions. The hip and knee cases point inward, and the final segment is a straight 105 mm link. The opposite tibia cheek is a removable bearing support, not a second lower-leg segment.

Use the parts from this release together. The earlier standalone bridge, 8 mm fixture spacers and temporary case-to-shaft gauge belong to the test fixture. They are not installed in this production-shaped leg. The integrated femur and tibia bridges now accept M3x30 screws through the removable cheek into captive nuts.

## Before assembly

Remove the exposed tree supports and the outside brim. Keep the flat horn seats, nut slots and servo-contact surfaces clean. Insert a nut gently into an accessible pocket before adding a servo. The nominal pockets are 5.6 mm across flats and the side-entry slots are 2.7 mm thick, based on your approximately 5.2 mm by 2.2 mm nuts. The nut should slide in and resist turning. Do not hammer it into the new part. Inspect any previously hammered part for splits or white stress marks before reusing it in a load path.

The kit's 32.5 mm overall screw is consistent with M3x30 because its head accounts for approximately 2.5 mm. All screw lengths below are measured under the head. Use flat metal washers; split lock washers change the stack and are not substitutes. Keep the supplied C018 main and auxiliary discs with their matching centre retainers.

The lower yaw arm's four counterbores are now 8.2 mm diameter and accept your approximately 7.7 mm washers. The hip and knee auxiliary centre openings are 8.4 mm diameter. The stated screw engagements assume 0.5 mm flat washers. Their actual thickness still needs checking. There are no heat-set inserts in this leg.

## 1. Mount the yaw servo

Place the yaw servo in `yaw_case_saddle`. Slide its three nuts into the outer end-wall pockets and seat them against the inner stops. Fit `case_cap_yaw` on the opposite side with three M3x30 screws and washers. Tighten evenly until the case is held. The cap is designed with a closure gap; do not crush it shut to make the seam disappear.

Attach the saddle's four feet to the bench plate with four M3x18 screws, one washer under each nut, and nuts underneath. The screw heads bear directly on the printed feet: omit top washers because the 7.7 mm washers would touch the servo clamp. Tighten these fixture screws gently. Clamp the back of the plate to a rigid bench. The bench edge should lie behind X=-50 mm in the model, leaving the servo, its underside screws, cable and moving arm hanging clear beyond the edge. The four larger holes are optional restraint points for an external fixture; their bolts are not part of the M3 kit.

## 2. Assemble the coxa and hip servo

Load the two staggered nuts through the side openings near the bottom of `coxa_upper_and_hip_saddle`. Place the hip servo in its inward-facing saddle and fit `case_cap_hip` with three M3x30 screws. The narrow rear cap ear sits beside the coxa post.

Centre and identify the yaw servo before fixing the horn orientation. Fit the main C018 horn and centre retainer, then attach the coxa's upper plate to its four peripheral holes with M3x6 screws and one 0.5 mm washer each. These screws pass through 4 mm of plastic and engage approximately 1.5 mm into the main flange.

On the opposite yaw face, fit the auxiliary disc with its matching centre retainer and the specified OD7.7 x 0.5 mm washer against the fixed stub. The disc must remain able to turn; its centre screw must not clamp the disc to the case.

Place all four printed `yaw_auxiliary_spacer` pieces between the disc and the lower arm. They are nominally 11.6 mm long. Fit four M3x20 peripheral screws through two 0.5 mm washers in each lower-arm counterbore, through the arm and spacer, into the auxiliary disc. Fit the two additional M3x20 screws through the lower arm into the post's staggered nuts. Tighten the bridge screws evenly and confirm free rotation. Do not mix the old 3.8, 4.8 or 8 mm fixture spacers into this stack.

## 3. Fit the femur and knee servo

Insert the femur bridge nut down through its top slot. Fit the knee servo into the integrated saddle on `femur_main_and_knee_saddle`, with the case tail pointing inward toward the hip. Fit `case_cap_knee` with three M3x30 screws, nuts and washers.

The femur main cheek attaches to the hip's main C018 horn using four M3x6 screws with one 0.5 mm washer each. Fit the hip auxiliary disc and its centre retainer as above, then bring `femur_auxiliary_cheek` onto that disc. Its small stand-off lands face inward toward the disc. Use four M3x8 screws with two 0.5 mm washers each. The peripheral engagement is approximately 1.9 mm in the 2.1 mm auxiliary flange; changing washer thickness changes that clearance.

Fit one M3x30 screw and washer through the auxiliary cheek into the femur bridge's captive nut. The bridge is integral with the main cheek. It does not require a second screw from the opposite side or the old external 8 mm spacers.

## 4. Fit the straight tibia

Insert two nuts through the top slots in the tibia bridge. Attach the main tibia cheek to the knee main horn using four M3x6 screws with one washer each. Fit the knee auxiliary disc, centre retainer and opposite tibia cheek. Use four M3x8 screws with two washers each at the auxiliary disc. Close the fork with two M3x30 screws and washers through the auxiliary cheek into the two captive bridge nuts.

The STL retains the internal filename `tibia_curved_main_and_foot` for traceability. Its geometry is the straight lower leg shown in the preview. The toe's small slot is for a later grip pad or strap; a bare PLA toe is not a validated walking foot.

## 5. Route and check the cables

Connect the three servos through their bus connectors using the existing compatible controller and power system. Connectors remain on the auxiliary face. Route the cables away from the horn discs, bridge screws and the path between a rotating cheek and the stationary case. Leave a flexible loop at each joint. The CAD reserve uses your 1.5 mm extra connector projection and 2 mm clearance; the actual cable loop still needs inspection through the motion range.

With power off, check the assembled motion gently without forcing the servo gears. Verify that no centre retainer locks an auxiliary disc, no screw tip touches the case and no cable pulls tight. A fixed case clamp and a rotating horn bracket perform different jobs. The temporary case-to-horn gauge would lock them together and must be absent.

## First powered check

Restrain the bench plate and leave the toe unloaded. Confirm unique servo IDs, direction and centre positions before commanding motion. Start with small, slow moves near the displayed pose. The sampled model limits are yaw -19 to +19 degrees, hip 16 to 34 degrees and knee 70 to 91 degrees. They are model coordinates, not raw servo commands. Native Fusion joint values are offsets from the displayed neutral pose of 0, 30 and 81 degrees.

The included simulation tests a suspended leg with ideal motors. It does not qualify printed strength, screw preload, servo temperature or robot balance. Inspect the nut entries, coxa post, bridge roots and layer surfaces after the first slow movement. Full-body loading and impact tests come after that inspection.
