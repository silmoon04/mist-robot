# Revised first fit bed

The actual first fit bed is ready to slice: `MIST_BED1_PROXIMAL_ALL_PARTS.3mf`. It contains seven objects inside 216 x 216 x 22 mm. The STEP roundtrip and independent STL/3MF checks passed, with no overlapping objects. `BED1_PROXIMAL_TOP_VIEW.png` is the measurement guide; the watermarked probe images are superseded drafts.

The coupon profile is fixed in `interface_contract.json` and remains physically unmeasured. The new leg source now adopts its bearing and keeper dimensions; confirmation against completed leg solids is still pending. This is a fit bed, not a release of the leg structure.

The candidate retains the original seven handled objects and their interfaces. Object 06 gains the long datum frame, three revised carrier seats, a keeper, an 8 mm sleeve/bolt-pattern wafer, a sleeve ladder, a horizontal nut/plate pocket and a slider slot. The original bed and its blank feedback are preserved.

Keep the arranged orientations and centre the whole bed as one group. A 225 x 225 mm usable bed leaves 4.5 mm per edge for all brim and edge allowance combined. Check the actual slicer preview, including its purge line. The 3MF contains geometry and millimetre units, not printer settings. Use normal layer-by-layer printing. The model volume is 113.93 cm3, about 141 g of fully solid PLA or 145 g of fully solid PETG; use the slicer's actual material estimate.

Use the intended final material, nozzle, layer height, walls, flow and XY compensation. The new bearing bores print vertically. The jack nut pocket deliberately prints horizontally, matching its intended carrier orientation. Inspect its small bridges in the slicer. Do not drill, ream or sand a test surface before recording its first result.

Measure the actual hardware first. Record bearing OD and width, shaft OD, sleeve OD/ID/length, screw-head dimensions and nut across-flats/length. Leave unavailable measurements blank.

1. **Measure the long frame before cutting any tabs.** Pads A, B and C are marked on the top-view guide. A-B checks X; C-B checks Y. Measure above the 3 mm-high rail. The nominal outside span is 144.5 mm and the inside span 134.5 mm. For each axis calculate `(outside + inside)/2`, which should be 139.5 mm. Keep the frame flat, use light jaw pressure and repeat each reading. Record both raw spans; do not replace them with the nominal values.
2. **Try the actual carrier seats.** The three added seats are labelled 25, 40 and 70 for 22.25, 22.40 and 22.70 mm. They have an OD34 body, 1.2 mm floor and 7.2 mm straight seat with no lead-in. Start with the largest. Select a clean hand slip fit that reaches the shoulder and can be removed. Record the two measured bore directions and depth, plus tightness, rubbing, cracks or whitening. Test a second bearing from the same batch. The original chamfered OD22 row has a different surrounding section and remains a separate fit family.
3. **Check the keeper and shaft hardware together.** Cut only the keeper's thin exterior tab; keep its working ring and holes intact. Install the keeper with suitable measured M3 hardware and verify that it retains the outer race while the inner race and real shaft turn freely. Use the actual inner-race spacers and end retainers when available. A printed shaft-clearance hole does not establish the fit between a metal shaft and a bearing bore, and this coupon does not set rotor endfloat.
4. **Fit the sleeves and bolt circle.** The three 8 mm-deep sleeve bores are 6.55, 6.60 and 6.75 mm, identified by one, two and three dots. Test the actual OD6.5 mm metal sleeves. After cutting the wafer's thin tab, fit the four sleeves, actual screws and matching metal pulley face. Record sleeve flushness on both faces, simultaneous bolt alignment, head access and interference with the shaft/spacer. The 8.2 mm centre bore leaves only a 0.6 mm inner ligament beside the sleeve seats. This is an unloaded fit specimen; it does not establish a printed carrier's torque capacity or the bore quality of a machined metal gear.
5. **Check the horizontal nut, plate and slider.** Cut the jack block's exterior tab before testing it; do not twist the block off the long gauge. Insert the actual coupling nut along the horizontal pocket, and insert the actual load plate through its side opening. Record binding and access. Check the separate slider slot with the actual bolt and spreader. The original shallow nut traps cannot substitute for this long pocket.
6. **Record the retained interfaces too.** The original bearing ladders, insert/bolt/nut/shaft board, servo-width gauges, shaft-clamp jaws and 624 keeper remain available. Follow the original coupon README for their assembly. The servo gauge checks width; case dimensions, horn geometry, cable exit and clamp preload also need direct hardware or assembly checks. The belt ticks show pitch spacing, not tooth engagement.

Use `feedback_BED1_proximal.csv`, which has 87 blank feature rows, including the original 74 unchanged records. Use `assembly_and_scale_feedback.csv` for the individual span readings and multi-part assembly observations. `FEEDBACK_MAPPING.md` explains which rows the existing refinement tools support. Scale, keeper and bolt-circle rows are manual checks, not simple bore corrections.

Record failures as well as successful fits. Do not copy CAD numbers into measured columns. Keep the same relevant material and process for the second round, which should contain only unresolved interfaces and one representative assembled stack. No second bed or measured results have been generated.

Fit results do not release the robot's gears, load-bearing structure or powered motion. The original printed 20T gear stages have separate unresolved tooth/web strength limits.
