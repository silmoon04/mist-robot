# Independent engineering review

Review date: 6 September 2026. Scope: `simulation/model.py`, `simulation/run_sim.py`, the analytic model and saved trajectories, wheel calculations, and the body CAD recipe available during the review. This is a review of an engineering candidate. It does not release a leg for powered use or establish stair capability. No hardware was operated. The reviewer changed only this report; findings were sent to the implementation agents.

The current reviewed mass is **3.95376294555 kg**, after reconciling the body CAD mass and COM. Stand, fixed-base leg swing, stance height, driving and ideal-range obstacle stopping pass their model criteria. The current 2:1 hip crawl and both step attempts fail. The 3.5:1 hip variant passes its slow crawl case, but this does not establish a manufactured transmission or stair capability.

The coupled transmission mathematics and nominal coordinate frames checked correctly. The review also found and helped correct an obstacle-test false pass, an inconsistent descent direction and incomplete contact acceptance criteria. Command hysteresis remains an approximation of physical backlash. Earlier **3.884 kg** results are historical evidence, preserved in `simulation/outputs_baseline_preCAD`; they are not the current design results.

## Checks that passed

- Nine analytic unit tests passed, including the numerical Jacobian, inverse kinematics, virtual work, coupled hip/knee motion, motor-travel mapping and affine mass allowance.
- Across 100 additional random vectors, ideal virtual work differed by at most **1.78e-15**: `(A.T @ motor_torque) dot qdot = motor_torque dot (A @ qdot)`. For the absolute-tibia belt, the hip motor load depends on `joint_hip_torque - joint_knee_torque`, as implemented.
- An isolated URDF load matched analytic wheel-link positions within **9.3e-9 m** at the neutral pose. Bullet reports the base inertial frame at the body COM; `base_link_pose` correctly removes its translated COM offset for the current zero-rotation inertial frame. The summed URDF mass and analytic mass both equal **3.884 kg** for the reviewed parameters.
- Default Bullet velocity motors are disabled before torque control. The controller applies `A.T @ (eta * shaft_torque)` to joint torques. Same-direction motoring torque falls toward zero at the assumed no-load speed, and peak torque is capped separately. It does not apply full stall torque at full no-load speed.
- Wheel inertia uses the axle as the local Y axis, consistent with the rotated cylinder. Body and link inertias remain approximate distributions: products of inertia, motor rotor inertia and actual CAD inertia are absent. These omissions limit acceleration, tipping and resonance predictions.
- The wheel study's **1,620 cases** reproduce. Four-contact acceleration at 4 kg requires **0.045297 N m** for 70 mm wheels and **0.055003 N m** for 85 mm wheels. The 15-degree slope figures are **0.098781** and **0.119949 N m**; two-contact loads double. Speed-increase ratios, efficiency penalties and the reported torque ceilings are consistent. No current-limit pass in the existing grid has zero predicted rpm.

The earlier independently rechecked **3.884 kg** baseline used `run_sim.py` SHA-256 `d95047507b5130b1a2d4e31166c1c14d411596f97fc1e2ed4900f1634aa8148b`, `model.py` SHA-256 `be18e5074f94d457e4a3b7b01fd80f1f89011537f824c023eb1170f38f6aad78`, and canonical parameter hash `043364ca09833c05f8d711fda6b7a7de019ae3bcbe12c949575a077214699b70`. Those hashes and the 170.0 mm independently replayed obstacle separation in the historical findings identify the earlier evidence, not the current mass-adjusted run.

## Limits that remain material

The wheel calculation combines nominal no-load current with linearly voltage-scaled no-load speed. That is a useful endpoint approximation, but not one internally consistent resistance/back-EMF model with constant friction current. An endpoint-consistent alternative changes the relevant FIT0493 70 mm acceleration estimate from about 1.073 to 1.066 m/s and the STS speed-increase 85 mm estimate from 1.067 to 1.053 m/s. It does not reverse the present comparison. Add voltage feasibility and a target-speed condition before reusing the current-limit Boolean as an overall operating pass.

The collision model uses boxes and cylinders, excludes parent/child contacts and additionally excludes each femur/wheel pair. It does not contain the real servo pods, belt runs, shafts, retainers, cables or cosmetic covers. Zero coarse contacts establishes no interference only for the checked proxy pairs and sampled trajectory. It cannot establish a collision-free, manufacturable leg. The native helper correctly raises on failed features and no-effect cuts; the offline body exporter records some such defects instead, so the two replay paths need a clean common recipe before native execution can be inferred.

The support polygon is a quasi-static diagnostic. It does not prove dynamic stability, traction or stair-edge support. Whole-run servo RMS is a heat-related indicator under an assumed rating, not a thermal model. An eight-second PASS does not establish continuous duty. The 180 mm step case uses one platform, not a repeated staircase with 270 mm treads, riser noses, friction variation or a perception/planning stack.

Before advancing beyond a candidate, retain the corrected failures, integrate the final leg geometry and its actual transmission mass, check the complete motion envelope, and identify the first assembled leg's backlash, force response and thermal behavior. Repeat matching-source simulations when those inputs change. Coupon feedback can establish fits; it cannot establish these load and motion properties.
