R5 straight lower leg

The knee-to-toe beam is now straight, with the toe centre still 105 mm from
the knee axis. The shank has a symmetric taper in side view and is 14 mm
wide across the robot. It is 23 mm deep at the fork and tapers to 10 mm at
the rounded toe. A small relief beside the knee clears the femur at deep
bend; it does not offset the central shank.

The previous curved fork and foot were already one printed main body. Its
curved outline came from the reference styling and the earlier raised
cross-bridge arrangement. A curved outline is not necessary for the joint
kinematics: those use joint axes and the toe position. Straightening the
member makes the geometric load path easier to inspect, but it does not
remove the need to check holes, layer direction, fork stiffness and loads.

The new shank and main horn cheek also form one continuous printed body.
A separate short auxiliary cheek supports the servo's opposite shaft. Both
printed pieces belong to one rigid tibia link, with no extra joint between
them. Keeping this cheek separate lets it print flat and makes the horn
hardware accessible during assembly and service. A fully fused fork may
be assemblable, but in the chosen broad-face print orientation its second
cheek would need extensive support across the 38 mm shaft-support gap.

The cross-bridge spans x=32 to 48 mm and z=-7 to +7 mm in tibia coordinates.
Its two M3 bolts remain parallel to the knee shaft, at (x,z)=(36,0) and
(44,0) mm. The bolt centres are 8 mm apart. The assembled fork remains
46 mm wide, including two 4 mm cheeks. Existing bolt lengths, washers,
nuts, 1.1 mm auxiliary lands and the 7.8 mm auxiliary centre access remain.

The local CAD check found no solid collisions in 304 interpart and cable
checks covering integer knee angles from 70 to 91 degrees. Six additional
cross-bolt shaft and washer access probes also cleared the printed parts.
Each printed part is one valid solid. These sampled results do not prove
every configuration.

Mesh-derived solid PLA estimates at 1.24 g/cm3 change from 36.359 g to
42.657 g per leg for the main body and auxiliary cheek together. The
increase is about 6.30 g per leg. Actual sliced mass is measured separately
from the production toolpath and will differ from this solid estimate.

The isolated main-cheek root sweep over x=11 to 31.5 mm improves its
minimum area from 80.144 to 84.778 mm2. Minimum section modulus about Y
changes from 267.629 to 299.470 mm3; about Z it changes from 37.204 to
56.519 mm3. The improvement is not uniform: at x=11 mm both section
moduli are slightly smaller, and the foot-strap opening at x=100 mm has a
smaller section than before. The bending moment near the toe is smaller
than at the knee, so section dimensions alone do not determine adequacy.

The actual bridge section at y=8 mm has net area 201.39 mm2 and section
modulus about X of 493.56 mm3, including the two M3 holes and corner
chamfers. The independent strength review uses that measured section with
the final robot's simulated loads. Neither this geometry study nor the
mesh section comparison establishes the strength of a printed PLA part.

Use the supplied main-body orientation of -90 degrees about X and the
auxiliary-cheek orientation of +90 degrees about X. These put the broad
cheek faces on the bed. The central shank needs local support in this
orientation because it sits above the main cheek's bed plane. Its support
amount and accessibility are checked by the production slicing audit.

The unresolved physical checks include actual horn/case fit, layer
adhesion, bolt preload, creep and lateral force sharing through the
opposite shaft. Those remain assembly and load-test measurements.
