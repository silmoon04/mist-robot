The exported R2 LANDSCAPE robot has reasonable sagittal bending margins in this conditional calculation, but its thin transverse connections and phone retainers deserve reinforcement before printing the complete assembly. The rigid body walking result does not establish printed strength. This report evaluates the existing meshes without changing CAD. Source hashes in strength_results.json identify the evaluated geometry; later geometry requires a new assessment.

The calculation uses the actual FL meshes, whose dimensions are shared by all four legs, and the 64 second trajectory. The largest recorded foot normal is 11.953 N at 54.15 s on RR. The largest recorded actuator torque is 0.529199 Nm at 51 s on hip FL. The robot CAD mass is 2.410992 kg. The frozen envelope adds 11.826 N per leg for equal two leg static support, 23.652 N per leg for two leg support at 2g, a 5 N side force per leg and a separate 5 N phone push. These added loads are design assumptions, not events demonstrated by the trajectory. Two feet do not provide a stable support polygon, and the torque cap may prevent a proposed pose from supporting these loads.

assess_strength.py is runnable with Python, numpy, trimesh and shapely. It intersects the exported STL triangles with planes and integrates polygon area and second moments, including voids and product inertia. The output records section dimensions, equations, assumptions and conditional margins. No continuum finite element solver, bead geometry or interlayer damage model was run. This is a beam and connection screen of the printed design, not an as-sliced failure simulation.

The material assumptions deliberately distinguish directions. Prusa's PLA data sheet reports 17 +/- 3 MPa interlayer adhesion using its own method, 51 +/- 3 MPa horizontal tensile yield on printed specimens, and a 55 C heat deflection temperature. Its printed tests used 0.20 mm layers, two perimeters and 100% rectilinear infill. Those data are useful context but do not characterize this spool or Neptune 4 Pro. The 57 MPa filament value and the sheet's vertical xz specimen label must not be substituted for tensile strength normal to layers. Source: [Prusament PLA technical data sheet, 2022](https://prusament.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PLA_Prusament_TDS_2021_10_EN.pdf).

For room temperature short duration screening, the script assumes 12 MPa in-plane tension, 4 MPa layer-normal tension, 3 MPa shear and 8 MPa projected hole bearing. These are engineering screening values, not guaranteed allowables or a fitted orthotropic constitutive law. It multiplies solid nominal stress by an assumed notch factor of 2 and divides by an effective area/inertia fraction of 0.5. The latter is conditional on adequate skins and material distribution; it is not a conversion of 35% gyroid to 50% strength. Exact joint notches can be worse than the assumed factor. Margin means assumed allowable divided by screening demand. A number below one is a reason to revise or test that load path; a number above one does not certify the assembly.

At 0.20 mm layer height, specify at least five top and five bottom solid layers for the initial coupons and inspect the slice. A 4 mm cheek printed on its broad side has its thickness along build Z: five perimeters do not fill that thickness automatically. One millimetre of skin on each broad face provides half of its solid cross-sectional area before infill, but stiffness credit still depends on the bending direction and actual toolpaths. Five 0.4 mm contour lines can consume thin walls lying within the build plane; variable line width, holes and slicer gap fill change that result. Retain the requested five walls and 35% gyroid as the starting process, with these explicit skin settings. Do not reduce the CAD mass by multiplying it by 0.35. Export sliced mass for every SKU and update robot mass and inertias before rerunning locomotion.

The strongest direction should follow the long bending fibres. Femur and tibia cheeks already rotate +/-90 degrees about X, putting their XZ outlines in the bed plane. This is appropriate for sagittal bending. It puts the long cross bridges along assembly Y across the print layers, where bending tension can split them. The coxa post also lies within the layer plane with the main coxa orientation, but the post's rectangular neck and nut slots remain stress raisers. The lower yaw arm prints flat in XY; its 4 mm depth gives poor out-of-plane stiffness. Printing the phone rear frame on its broad back keeps the upright fibres in-plane, but its USB clearance cuts the rear spines down to 2 mm thickness.

The paired cheeks and bolted bridges may act together under side load. That stiffness and load sharing depend on screw fit, contact and bridge deformation. The table intentionally gives the main cheek the entire lateral moment; it does not claim that the complete yoke necessarily fails at 5 N. Crediting both cheeks as a perfect box would be equally unjustified without connection compliance. The lower yaw arm likewise receives the whole vertical envelope in its bounding case. Its actual load fraction must be measured or obtained from an assembled contact model. These bounds identify where that uncertainty matters.

For the horn group the script uses force per bolt T/(4r)+F/4, projected bearing F/(dt), net-section bending M/Z and approximate nut-face punching F/(perimeter times remaining thickness). It does not apply a guessed installation torque. Fastener preload can exceed operating load and split the thin pocket face before walking starts. The cap end bolt at x=17 has only 3.5 mm to the x=20.5 end, leaving a 1.8 mm edge ligament beyond the 3.4 mm hole. Its tear-out shear screen is 7.48 MPa against the assumed 3 MPa. The yaw mounting foot strips score a conditional margin of 1.14. The open cheek torsion estimate, 3T/(b t squared), gives 2.62 MPa for 5 N acting 21 mm off centre, against 3 MPa assumed shear. An isolated 4 x 30 mm femur cheek Euler comparison with effective E=1.2 GPa, 50% inertia and a conservative cantilever effective length gives 29.24 N. The 23.65 N compression envelope is too close to credit buckling casually; real end restraint, curved profiles and paired cheeks govern.

Reinforcement should target these details first:

1. Thicken the phone's rear USB spines rearward. Preserve the measured plug envelope at x>=-2.5 while adding material behind x=-4.5, and round the transition above and below the opening. Check the full pan sweep after changing the back. Replace or deepen the long front retaining bars where screen access permits. Their calculated stresses are misuse screens; phone contact and distributed loading may reduce them, but the current bars should not be handles.
2. Increase the femur bridge section, add generous end radii or triangular webs and retain the auxiliary fastening so both cheeks share load. A separately printed transverse spacer with fibres along its length is an alternative if integral bridge layer tension remains high. Support removal must not damage its root. Keep the x=74 screw access and servo insertion clearance reviewable.
3. Preserve more solid wall behind the nut pockets around tibia bolts x=48 and 55, z=18, and femur bolt x=74, z=-13. Add an external boss, washer land or through nut arrangement where horn and servo clearance permits. A deeper nut recess without a thicker boss makes this worse.
4. Rib or deepen the yaw lower arm near the post, and radius the post transition at x=28..36.5. Rework the nut loading slots at z=-39.5..-36.9 so they do not remove half of the post's section at one height. Keep the auxiliary shaft clearance, assembly access and lower arm screw stack intact.
5. Add local radii and bearing lands to cap ends and saddle mounting feet. Then compare side-loaded yoke deflection with sagittal deflection before deciding whether the long cheeks need ribs. Simply increasing every wall increases servo demand without resolving the weak connections.

Lightening belongs after those changes. The broad cover panels, tray faces and central deck areas away from mounting holes are candidates for pockets or shallow ribs. The cover carries the phone through its top pads, walls and four mounting bosses; it is not cosmetic. The deck carries yaw brackets across openings; a uniform unsupported plate estimate would miss the ligament routes. Preserve material around pan pads, yaw feet, cover bosses, battery tabs and cable-slot corners. No deck, cover or battery-tray strength pass is claimed here. Do not pocket the 4 mm cheeks, 3 mm feet or nut lands based on the sagittal margin alone. Compare grams saved with the resulting centre of mass and motor torque, using actual sliced mass.

Printed PLA also relaxes and creeps under sustained strain, so screw clamping and long standing poses need time-based tests. Thermal history and raster direction affect the behaviour in [Stress Relaxation Behavior of Additively Manufactured PLA](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/15/10/3509). Long-term creep below the glass transition is documented in [Effect of physical aging on flexural creep in printed thermoplastic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11240). Treat 55 C heat deflection as a test result rather than an acceptable servo-adjacent service temperature. The present margins apply only to short duration, approximately room temperature loads; no warm or long-term allowable has been established. Measure clamp and enclosed body temperatures during the eventual powered run.

Before final geometry approval, print the existing fit coupons plus bridge, nut-pocket and USB-spine representatives in the intended orientations with the same spool and settings. Test at least three XY and three layer-normal coupons if quantitative allowables are needed. Use a fixture and incremental loads on sacrificial parts: 12 N then 24 N foot-equivalent vertical load, 5 N side load, and 0.588 Nm horn torque, with the planned screw stack. Hold the assembled unpowered leg at its working load for an hour and again for a day; record deflection, residual set, whitening, cracks and nut movement. Test with a dummy phone mass and controlled 5 N retainer push before installing the real phone. These targets are proof-plan proposals, not completed tests or life qualification. Stop at cracking or permanent set. Recheck warm parts at the measured service temperature. Coupon results, actual slice settings, screw compliance and an assembled joint fixture are the next evidence needed to replace the conditional assumptions.
