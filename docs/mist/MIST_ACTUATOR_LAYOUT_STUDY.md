# MIST actuator-layout study — 2026-07-09

Generated by `sim/` (Config study button; full illustrated version downloads as
`MIST_CONFIG_REPORT.html`). Five placements of the knee + wheel servos, each
re-optimized over femur/tibia/hip-offset/azimuths/stance/height under identical
hard constraints: worst servo ≤35% of stall at 9.9 V, tip ≥12°, leg branch
≤10.8 A, flex ≤0.8 mm, standing reach ≤86% of leg length (stride headroom).
Scored on a pose battery: stand, 55% squat, one-leg lift (centroid-shifted),
10° slope. Torque model verified by 13 invariant self-tests.

### Fixed-wheel heading caveat (added 2026-07-12)

The study optimizes loads and pose geometry; it does not resolve the no-slip
kinematics of four conventional fixed wheels. In the current chain each wheel
axis remains knee-parallel, so its nominal rolling direction is radial to the
fixed body mount. The four Layout A mount rays at ±25°/±109° do not give four
parallel wheel headings for ordinary translation or a standard steering
geometry for yaw. A simulator “drive stance” that changes mount azimuth is not
physically achievable with fixed pods.

Before freezing wheel carriers and chassis mounts, choose and test one of:
low-speed compliant-tire scrub/skid; omni/caster-style contact; an added yaw
steering DOF; or mechanically adjustable pod azimuth. This caveat does not
invalidate the relative actuator-load comparison, but it is a separate mobility
architecture gate.

## Reading the numbers (sanity reasoning)

- **Every layout passes the constraint set** once geometry is re-optimized —
  actuator placement is a *quality* choice on this robot, not a feasibility one.
  The 30 kg·cm servos are strong enough everywhere; crawl 3-leg phases (34–42%)
  are the sizing case, standing is trivial (7–12%).
- **Swing torque is where layouts truly differ** (2.5× spread): A/B carry
  ~0.125 N·m per hip swing, C/E ~0.05. That's gait speed, smoothness, and
  battery under motion — invisible when standing.
- **Knee-mounting the wheel servo raises hip load in sprawl** (B swing-hip
  worst at 0.128): the knee sits farther out than the foot, so mass at the knee
  has a *longer* gravity arm. Counter-intuitive but verified by the
  finite-difference torque self-test.
- A's optimizer went long-femur (111 mm) because with all mass distal anyway,
  femur length buys tallest-stand (171 mm) cheaply. E's huge d1 (59 mm) exists
  to package 4 servos at the hip — the optimizer uses it as a stance widener
  (best slope, 30°).
- Slope numbers ≥25° flatter the model: belt/linkage friction and TPU tire
  squirm aren't modeled. Trust the ordering, not the last 5°.
- Numbers shift a few % between optimizer runs (different local optima of equal
  quality); the ORDERING of layouts on swing torque, mass, and crawl load is
  stable.

## Buildability ranking (printed-parts reality)

1. **A — all direct**: zero transmissions. Print brackets, done. 110 g of servo
   at/below each knee; wheel servo eats ground impacts; wires run to feet.
2. **B — wheel@knee**: one belt per leg spanning NO moving joint (knee→axle
   along the rigid tibia) — easiest transmission there is. Needs printed
   pulleys + tensioner + a free axle on a bearing.
3. **C — knee@hip linkage**: classic pushrod/four-bar, ratio 1.5 multiplies
   knee torque, cuts knee ROM equally. Every printed pivot adds backlash. 3
   servos to package per hip.
4. **D — C+B combined**: two transmissions per leg; lightest below-knee mass;
   most printed precision parts.
5. **E — all@hip**: wheel belt must cross the MOVING knee (idler coaxial with
   the knee axis) — hardest mechanism, best mass layout, cleanest wiring
   (everything at the hip).

## Recommendation

- **Build v1 as A (all direct)** — it passes everything, and MIST's actual risk
  right now is printed-bracket quality, not torque. Zero transmission tuning.
- **Design the tibia so B is a drop-in retrofit** (leave room for a belt run +
  axle bearing): B is the cheapest meaningful upgrade — unsprung mass halves,
  and it's the only transmission that doesn't cross a joint.
- Skip C/D/E for v1: their swing-torque win (real!) only pays once you're
  chasing faster gaits, and each adds a mechanism that can eat weeks of print
  iteration. Revisit E for v2 if walking dynamics become the bottleneck.
