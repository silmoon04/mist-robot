# Simulation review, Sol 03

Reviewed `mist_env.py`, `gait.py`, `servo.py`, `model.py` and `evaluate.py`. No core source edits or physical actuation. The default simulation has no demonstrated P0/P1 blocker in these checks. The frame and friction corrections are sound; the attitude-sign change needs a clearer control objective.

This is the snapshot before Sol 04's delayed sensor integration (`mist_env.py` SHA-256 prefix `273e3c9a7ddc`, `gait.py` `ad2943dbba22`). The dynamic sign tests use the immediate nominal attitude interface present in that snapshot; repeat them after sensor latency is integrated. The report does not claim to validate the pending sensor implementation.

## Findings

1. **P2: several exposed configuration fields have no effect.** `servo.py:18-23,32,46-51` hardcodes voltage, gain/deadband ranges, ambient temperature, derating and thermal rates instead of reading their corresponding `config.json` fields. A runtime config override requesting exactly 7 V, gain scale 2, deadband 1 degree and ambient 30 C still produced 11.12484 V, gain scales 0.8749 to 1.1870, deadbands 0.1418 to 0.4697 degrees and 25 C. Defaults currently match most literals, so existing default results are unaffected. Calibration or sensitivity edits would silently fail. Read the declared ranges and thermal fields, validate them once, and add a test with deliberately different values. `mist_env.py:68,124` similarly ignores configured restitution, and `:167` hardcodes gravity-vector noise.

2. **P2: position and velocity use different reference points.** `_orientation()` correctly converts the base COM/principal-inertia pose into the CAD body pose, but `_info()` (`mist_env.py:176-183`) reports COM linear velocity as `body_velocity`. Evaluation computes displacement from the CAD origin and velocity RMSE from that COM velocity. With COM velocity zero and world angular velocity `[0,1,0]` rad/s, the nominal CAD origin moves at about `[-0.03354,0,-0.00105]` m/s in body axes while `body_velocity` reports zero. The nominal COM is about 33.54 mm above the CAD origin. This can matter against a 0.01 m/s command during rocking. Choose one scoring reference; for the CAD origin use `v_origin = v_com - omega_world x (com_world - origin_world)` before rotating into body axes. Keeping COM velocity is also valid if positions and labels consistently use COM.

3. **P2 controller concern: inverse attitude rotation is not a demonstrated body-levelling improvement.** `gait.py:169` passes the world toe-height geometry test, but that test does not measure the closed-loop body response. I compared current transpose, the original positive rotation and no compensation at 1000 Hz, 50 Hz actor rate, nominal 0.155 kg phone and a 0.01 m/s forward command. After 1.5 s, each body received a +/-0.04 rad roll or pitch offset. Every variant recovered without falling. Mean final-one-second roll/pitch RMS was about 0.002943 rad for transpose, 0.001508 for positive rotation and 0.001962 without compensation. Separate 0.4 Nm, 0.1 s roll/pitch torque pulses also survived; transpose peaks were 0.00860/0.00918 rad versus 0.00765/0.00825 for positive rotation. These small nominal tests favour the original sign for levelling, but the absolute difference is small and does not establish a broad stability defect. Define whether this term should hold world foot locations or regulate body tilt, then test both signs over several gait phases and randomized held-out cases. Retain a dynamic regression test; do not justify the sign solely with toe-height geometry. Stop commands bypass attitude correction at `gait.py:140-141`.

4. **P3: the pass flag is a deliberately weak screening criterion.** `evaluate.py:43` accepts any upright stop episode, regardless of drift, and moving episodes need only 25% projected progress. Velocity error, lateral drift, yaw drift and saturation do not affect acceptance; yaw-rate commands are absent. A fake environment with 2 m forward/4 m lateral displacement in 0.04 s, 172 degrees yaw drift and full saturation still passes both stop and forward. This is a unit probe of the criterion, not a physical rollout. The file explicitly discloses its criterion, so this is not a hidden claim or a blocker for pipeline smoke tests. Before treating pass rate as walking validation, add stop speed/displacement limits, lateral/yaw bounds, minimum duration and yaw command cases, with thresholds chosen for the intended robot test.

## Checks and limits

- `work/py311/bin/python -m pytest outputs/mist-robot/locomotion/tests -q`: 23 passed, 4 subtests passed. Only Gymnasium warnings about unbounded observation boxes.
- Full nominal five-second forward rollouts at 1000 and 2000 Hz gave 0.031033 and 0.031145 m progress, 0.038072 and 0.038171 rad final mean tracking error, and no falls. This supports that short contact rollout in addition to the existing single-leg convergence evidence. Reset settling currently uses 120 physics ticks, so its duration differs between rates; neither this check nor the documented leg sweep proves arbitrary randomized-contact convergence.
- Runtime dynamics readback gave floor friction 1.0 and toe friction 0.8. [Bullet's primary source](https://github.com/bulletphysics/bullet3/blob/master/src/BulletCollision/CollisionDispatch/btManifoldResult.cpp#L49-L57) multiplies the two lateral coefficients, so the effective coefficient is correctly 0.8, not 0.64.
- The 47-value actor observation excludes world position, linear velocity, simulator contacts and randomized motor parameters. Scheduled support/gravity position bias uses nominal source masses, planned gait phase and known phone mass. I found no new contact or randomized-parameter leakage into the actor. Model support error from COM offsets, changing contacts and tilted gravity remains an approximation, as the code states.
- Mass/inertia scale together, mesh inertials convert millimetres to metres, and the nominal body frame conversion passes its runtime IMU test. Unmeasured solid-print mass, motor dynamics, primitive collisions and thermal parameters are already disclosed estimates, not newly discovered blockers.

Dynamic scripts and full histories are retained under `work/sol03/dynamic_review.py`, `dynamic_evidence.json` and `stronger_push_evidence.json`. The sign comparisons monkeypatch only the in-memory controller. No training was run.
