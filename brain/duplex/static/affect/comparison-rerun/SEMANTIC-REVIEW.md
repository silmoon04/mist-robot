# Semantic review of the affect comparison

This is a single pass over six text-only cases in `smoke.json` and `remaining.json`, plus the three-turn `../live-smoke/probe-20260924T164458Z-9061ff/probe-summary.json` and its public trace. The comparison reader proposals were validated but never applied to a face or voice. The live probe sent PCM packets through TTS; it did not verify browser playback or what a person heard.

## What the choices meant

The explicit curious-face case worked through speaker ownership: every speaker arm called `set_expression` for `curious`, with a persistent result. GPT-OSS correctly proposed no separate face change. Qwen proposed the same curious change despite the explicit user request being reserved for the speaker, so validation discarded it. Its late completion made no difference in this run. In the printer case, both readers preserved the requested neutral face. The Qwen speaker gave a useful diagnostic question; the GPT-OSS arm's speaker answer opened with "That's maddening," a stronger emotional judgment than the user asked the robot to show.

In the disappointment case, Qwen's sad face and gentle delivery were grounded in the user's report and could fit a sympathetic reply, though changing the face for an ordinary disclosure is more reactive than simply speaking gently. GPT-OSS made essentially the same proposal but omitted evidence. The validator correctly kept the neutral face and delivery. This is a structural failure, not proof that its intended affect was poor.

The correction case exposes the reverse distinction. GPT-OSS returned a schema-valid, grounded change to `sad` variant 1 with gentle delivery. The user had asked for sad, not an alternate variant; the face catalog warns that sad alternates can read theatrically. Qwen kept the already requested sad preset. All three speakers called `set_expression` for sad, but lines such as "Sad's up now" claim a visible repair when the tool receipt only says `display_requested`. The user had just reported a visual mismatch, so a request receipt should not be phrased as visual confirmation.

For "Can you not talk with a sad face, though?", Qwen's proposed change to the already current neutral face failed consistency validation; GPT-OSS held neutral. The two completed speaker replies both assumed the user meant "can you speak while showing sad" and answered yes. The wording could also mean "please don't speak with a sad face". A brief clarification would serve that ambiguity better. In the arithmetic case, both readers kept the previously requested sad face with neutral delivery. That is coherent: a factual answer need not sound sad because the face persists. Both speakers that completed said "Fifty-six." Two GPT-OSS-arm speaker requests, in the ambiguous question and arithmetic cases, ended in read timeouts with no reply. They cannot count as timely successes or support a causal claim about the reader.

## Timing and live speech

Against the first nonblank speaker text, Qwen finished in time on 3 of 6 valid speaker replies; GPT-OSS finished in time on 4 of 4 valid replies. Qwen had 4 of 6 structurally accepted decisions; GPT-OSS had 5 of 6. These counts describe this one pass, and the comparison did not apply proposals. In particular, the two GPT-OSS-arm speaker timeouts shrink its timing denominator to four. The first phrase boundary gives the same timely counts here, but production locks delivery at the earlier assistant turn start.

The live Qwen probe shows the practical limit. Turn 1 kept neutral reader delivery while the speaker selected a persistent curious face and gave a clear hinge definition. On turn 2, a grounded sad/gentle proposal finished after speech started; the recorded TTS style remained neutral and no new face packet was sent. The earlier curious face therefore remained the server's selected face during the setback. The speaker's short sympathy was reasonable. On turn 3, the reader correctly kept that face and suggested warm delivery, but again finished after speech started, so TTS stayed neutral. The speaker's encouragement, "A second attempt with the stand reinforced probably won't hurt as much as the first one did," predicts how the user will feel and lands poorly as encouragement. The affect reader cannot repair wording it does not own.

## Recommendation

Keep both reader options experimental. GPT-OSS is the better candidate for the next targeted trial because it was timely on the four completed speaker replies and respected the explicit curious-face ownership, but this sample does not establish better overall behavior: it omitted required evidence once, chose an unrequested sad variant, and two paired speaker replies failed. Test repeated turns with browser playback, explicit face corrections, and emotional disclosures before choosing a default. Score spoken wording and visible face separately from schema acceptance.
