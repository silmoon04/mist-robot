Qwen low starts text at about the same speed as GPT-OSS low in this run. Turning Qwen reasoning off saves roughly 100 ms on the overall median. Neither result establishes a general quality winner.

The 36 samples per setting repeat 12 cases. Timings exclude speech recognition and voice playback. Prompt cache hits were common across all four settings.

The two independent model judges saw shuffled answers without model names or timing. Each rated only the first repetition: 12 answers per setting. These are descriptive model ratings, not human ratings, pass rates or proof of a significant difference.

What still went wrong

  • Both Qwen settings sometimes treated an ambiguous word as a dictionary question instead of asking what it referred to.
  • GPT-OSS low changed a supplied year from 2031 to 2023 in one reviewed answer.
  • Both GPT-OSS settings offered overly specific forecasts from limited evidence. Correction carryover worked well in the reviewed answers.

Voice and animation fixes

  • Later audio chunks now use the reply-relative alignment offset. The same recorded audio went from 10 of 55 aligned packets to 55 of 55. A fresh generation also aligned all 57 packets.
  • The visible caption follows the speaker clock instead of the newest generated text. Missing alignment is reported, not replaced with invented timings.
  • Thinking starts after a 650 ms wait, fades out as speech starts, and uses a brief pulse with a longer hold. Existing faces and 15 fps artwork are retained.

Mouth shapes still approximate speech from letters and audio energy. Timing tests do not replace listening and judging how it feels. The live trial keeps the existing default; Qwen off, Qwen low and GPT-OSS high are separate choices.
